### B2. The steps are app output, not a hand drawing

`components/home/assets.ts` and the homepage copy describe this set as "the
real steps Sketch Steps generated from this portrait photo" (exported from the
app). If the article uses them, the text must say so (for example "the steps
Sketch Steps generated from a photo") and must not suggest a person drew them
or that the reader is watching an artist's process. That is honest and
workable, but it changes the article's framing, and the AI-assistance
disclosure question becomes more important.

### B3. Resolution is too low for the article

| Requirement (outline) | Supplied |
|---|---|
| Steps at least 900 px wide | 298-299 px wide |
| Featured image at least 1200 px wide (social cards) | Step 9 is 299×400 |

At 300 px, pencil lines look soft on phones and Retina screens once enlarged,
and the step cards would have to stay small. Upscaling cannot add line detail.
The original app exports at full resolution (ideally 900×1200 or larger) are
needed. The homepage already notes the same limit
(`STEP_DISPLAY_MAX_WIDTH`).

### B4. Framing is not consistent between steps

Measured earlier for the homepage demo (`stepFraming` in
`components/home/assets.ts`): steps 1-6 are drawn about 5-7% larger and
lower than steps 7-9 (up to ~23 px shift at this size). Shown as separate
cards, readers will see the head "jump" between steps 6 and 7. Fix: consistent
re-export, or the same per-step correction the homepage uses (CSS only).

### B5. Reference photo

Identical to the homepage demo photo. Kept **out** of the article until the
team confirms publication rights for this use, as decided.

## Step by step

### Step 1: The cranium ball
- **Added:** a single light circle in the upper middle of the page.
- **Loomis principle:** the head starts as a ball for the cranium.
- **Beginner focus:** draw it light and leave room below it for the jaw and
  chin (in the image, roughly the lower third of the page stays empty).
- **Common mistake:** a circle placed too low or too large, so the chin runs
  off the page. Fix: sketch the circle small first; size can be corrected
  while lines are light.
- Clear.

### Step 2: Center line and horizontal line
- **Added:** a vertical line through the circle that continues well below it,
  and a horizontal line across the circle. Both are slightly tilted, matching
  the tilt of the head in the photo.
- **Loomis principle:** the center line shows which way the face points and
  tilts; the horizontal line marks the brow line in the Loomis method.
- **Beginner focus:** the lines follow the tilt of the head; they are not
  forced to be perfectly vertical/horizontal. The vertical line extends below
  the circle because the face continues down to the chin.
- **Common mistake:** drawing the cross perfectly upright when the head is
  tilted, which makes every feature placed on it look off. Fix: match the
  angle of the head in the reference.
- **UNCLEAR (U1):** the horizontal line sits at about the middle of the
  circle. Is it meant as the brow line (standard Loomis) or the eye line? In
  step 4 the eyebrows sit just above it and the eye curves on it. Reviewer to
  confirm the term before the text names it.

### Step 3: Side planes and the jaw
- **Added:** curved vertical lines just inside the left and right edges of the
  circle, and an angular jaw: lines from the sides of the head down to a
  flat-bottomed chin, below the circle. A faint curve along the lower part of
  the circle remains visible.
- **Loomis principle:** the sides of the ball are "sliced off" (side planes),
  and the jaw hangs below the ball, ending at the chin on the center line.
- **Beginner focus:** the chin sits where the center line ends; the jaw is a
  few straight segments at this stage, not a smooth curve.
- **Common mistake:** a jaw that is too long or too narrow. Fix: compare the
  face length with the reference before adding features.
- **UNCLEAR (U2):** the side-plane marks are vertical arcs at the circle edge,
  not the oval side plane usually drawn in Loomis diagrams (normal for a
  front view, where the plane is seen almost edge-on). Reviewer to confirm
  wording.
- **UNCLEAR (U3):** there are faint diagonal lines inside the jaw area
  (left side, toward the chin). Purpose not clear (cheek line? earlier jaw
  attempt?). Not to be described unless confirmed.
- **Not shown:** explicit hairline, nose line or chin line marks. The text
  must not instruct readers to mark facial thirds here.

### Step 4: Placing the features
- **Added:** two more horizontal guidelines (one through the eye area, one
  just below the nose), eyebrows as outline shapes, the eyes as single curved
  lines (they look closed at this stage), the nose (tip and nostrils), and
  the mouth (line between the lips and a short mark for the lower lip). The
  jaw is redrawn slightly.
- **Loomis principle:** features are placed on the horizontal guidelines,
  centered on the center line, before any detail.
- **Beginner focus:** symmetry around the center line; the mouth sits closer
  to the nose than to the chin in this drawing; keep everything as simple
  shapes.
- **Common mistakes:** eyes placed too high on the head (beginners often draw
  them near the top; here they sit around the middle of the circle); features
  drawn too large. Fix: check positions against the guidelines.
- **UNCLEAR (U4):** whether the guidelines follow the Loomis thirds exactly
  (hairline / brow / nose line / chin) cannot be measured reliably at this
  resolution, and the hairline is never marked. The text will describe
  proportions as approximate and avoid claiming exact thirds.
- **UNCLEAR (U5):** the jaw outline differs from step 3 (redrawn, not
  traced). Fine for the article if described as "adjust the jaw if needed".

### Step 5: Hair shape, neck and shoulders
- **Added:** the outline of the hair mass above and around the head (wavy,
  with volume above the circle), the outer contour of the face/cheeks, the
  neck, and the T-shirt collar and shoulders. Eyebrows, eyes and mouth are
  still simple; the nose and smile are a little clearer.
- **Loomis principle:** big shapes before details; the hair sits on top of
  the cranium, so it rises above the circle.
- **Beginner focus:** draw the hair as one outer shape, not single strands;
  the hair extends above the circle (hair has volume).
- **Common mistake:** hair drawn tight to the circle (head looks too small) or
  strand by strand from the start. Fix: block in the whole silhouette first.
- **UNCLEAR (U6):** no ears are drawn in this or later steps, though the
  photo shows them. Either the hair covers them in this interpretation or they
  were left out. The text will not tell readers where to draw ears unless the
  team wants a note on it.

### Step 6: Eyes, eyebrows and facial details
- **Added:** open eyes with irises and pupils, eyebrows filled with strokes,
  a more defined nose, lips with a smile, smile lines at the corners of the
  mouth, more defined jaw and chin, first strands inside the hair. The hair
  outline is redrawn with a different, fuller shape than in step 5.
  Construction lines are lighter but still visible.
- **Loomis principle:** detail is added inside the structure that is already
  there.
- **Beginner focus:** the eyes take the most care; irises are partly covered
  by the upper lid in this drawing.
- **Common mistake:** pressing hard on the first detailed lines. Fix: keep
  them light enough to adjust.
- **UNCLEAR (U7):** the hair silhouette changes noticeably between steps 5
  and 6 (not just refined). Text can say "refine the hair shape"; reviewer
  to confirm that is acceptable.

### Step 7: Refined line drawing
- **Added (subtle):** more strands and flow lines in the hair, darker eye
  details, refined contours of the cheek, jaw and neck. Mostly line work, no
  real shading yet. Framing changes slightly (see B4).
- **Loomis principle:** none new; this is the clean line drawing on top of
  the construction.
- **Beginner focus:** follow the direction the hair grows in; line weight a
  little stronger on outer contours.
- **UNCLEAR (U8):** the difference from step 6 is small and hard to see at
  300 px. At article size it may look like a repeat. Higher-resolution files
  would help; otherwise the text should point to the hair as the main change.

### Step 8: Light shading
- **Added:** light hatching for the first tones: in the hair, along one side
  of the face and nose, under the cheekbones, on the neck and in the shirt.
- **Principle:** values (light and shadow) start light and cover the larger
  areas first.
- **Beginner focus:** decide where the light comes from and keep shadows on
  the same side.
- **Common mistake:** shading the whole face evenly. Fix: leave the lit areas
  white.
- **UNCLEAR (U9):** light direction is not obvious at this size (shadow
  looks slightly stronger on the viewer's right side of the face). Reviewer
  to confirm before the text names a side.

### Step 9: Finished sketch with darker values
- **Added:** darker hair with defined strands, deeper shadows on the face,
  under the jaw and on the neck, darker eyes and eyebrows, more contrast
  overall. Faint construction lines are still visible (forehead, center line).
- **Beginner focus:** darken gradually; add the darkest values last.
- **Common mistake:** going dark too early and losing the light areas. Fix:
  build up tone in layers.
- **UNCLEAR (U10):** the leftover construction lines in the final image:
  intentional (sketchbook look) or to be erased? The text should not tell
  readers to erase them unless the team confirms.
