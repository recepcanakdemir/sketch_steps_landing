## Uncertainties and problems

1. **Order of steps 3 and 4 (supplied as 29 and 22): high confidence, not certain.**
   - Image 22 has the long neck-to-wing line; image 29 does not.
   - The line is still present in image 21 (the legs), so 29 comes before 22.
   - The back line looks darker in 29 than in 22. That is most likely exposure: the photos differ in brightness.
2. **Large jump between step 5 and step 6.** Head details, the wing and tail edges, and the leg shapes all appear at once. The article breaks step 6 into five parts (head, neck and chest, wing, tail, legs) instead of inventing intermediate images.
3. **Framing differs between photos.** The paper is shifted or zoomed slightly from image to image; image 28 (step 7) is noticeably closer. Each step still reads correctly. No framing correction was applied.
4. **Grass in step 9:** it is not darker than in step 8 (measured: same average tone). The text does not claim it is.
5. **Low resolution.** Each image is about 341 px. If larger originals exist, replace the files and update the sizes in `content/blog/how-to-draw-a-turkey/index.ts`.
6. **Origin of the images is unconfirmed.** Hand-drawn, or generated by Sketch Steps? The face article discloses that its stages were generated by Sketch Steps, and the same rule applies here before publication.
7. **Materials:** only pencil and paper are visible. An eraser is implied, because the construction lines are gone in step 8, but this has not been confirmed.

## Suitability for beginners

- **Steps 1–5:** simple shapes and lines; suitable for beginners.
- **Steps 6–7:** a detailed line drawing; manageable with patience.
- **Steps 8–9:** realistic feather texture and dark values take time and control. This is intermediate work.

So "easy", "simple" and "cute" are not used. The drawing is a realistic wild turkey, not a cartoon, and it does not teach the hand-turkey technique.
